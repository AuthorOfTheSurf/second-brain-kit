# The Mastermind

> [!summary] A personal advisory board of people who've done what you're trying to do. Each one is modeled well enough that you can bring a real situation and ask, "How would they handle this?"

## The idea
A mastermind is normally a group of peers you think with. This one is on demand: pages about people you admire, written so the AI can answer in their voice. Facing a pricing call, ask for the advice of someone who's built offers for a living. Facing a hard negotiation, call in a negotiator.

Advice comes with a flavor. Two advisors often disagree, and the disagreement shows you the real tradeoff.

## Two ways to learn from someone
| | Teacher | Advisor |
|---|---|---|
| Use | learn their method, apply it yourself | bring your situation, get their verdict |
| Page needs | an accurate summary | an accurate summary *plus* their voice: how they think, their go-to moves, what they'd push back on |
| Lives in | `wiki/sources/` | `wiki/mastermind/` |

Someone can be both. A teacher graduates to an advisor once their page captures enough voice to answer in character.

## Adding an advisor
- Pick someone who has done the specific thing you're stuck on.
- Ask the AI to research them from primary sources: their books, interviews, talks.
- The page ends with a **How they'd advise you** section: their priorities, their questions, their usual moves.
- Mark what's from their own words and what's a summary.

## Calling them in
- "Ask my mastermind: should I raise prices or add a cheaper tier?"
- "What would [advisor] say about this plan? Then what would [other advisor] say?"

## Honest caveat
It's a model of the person, not the person. It's only as good as the sources behind the page, and it can sound confident when it's wrong. Use it to widen your options, not to outsource the decision.
